\section{Summary}
In Week~3 (and early Week~4 follow-through), the team completed all deliverables for Milestones \textbf{M3} and \textbf{M4} and finished the full training and evaluation of Proposed \textbf{Model~P} (\textbf{M5}). All three baseline families---audio-only \textbf{B1}, visual-only \textbf{B2} (v1 \& v2), and concatenation fusion \textbf{B3} (v1 \& v2)---along with all four initialisation variants of \textbf{Model~P} (bidirectional cross-modal attention + per-frame gated fusion + learned missing-modality embedding $\mathbf{e}_{\text{miss}}$) have been trained on the full GRID corpus on our Alienware RTX 4080 SUPER workstation and evaluated across all five acoustic conditions ($\text{clean}, 10, 5, 0, -5$\,dB) on the four held-out test speakers.

The empirical findings validate both our multimodal hypothesis and our architectural choices:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{B1 (Audio-Only)} achieves $1.96\,\%$ test WER on clean speech ($1.76\,\%$ with grammar decoding), but degrades nearly five-fold under severe noise ($9.45\,\%$ greedy, $8.35\,\%$ grammar at $-5$\,dB).
  \item \textbf{B2 (Visual-Only)} is completely invariant to acoustic noise ($23.04\,\%$ greedy, $21.03\,\%$ grammar test WER across all SNRs for v1), providing a noise-immune floor in adverse environments.
  \item \textbf{B3 (Concatenation Fusion v2)} reduces B1's error at every SNR (\textbf{1.16\,\%} clean, \textbf{5.21\,\%} at $-5$\,dB greedy; \textbf{0.77\,\%} clean, \textbf{3.83\,\%} at $-5$\,dB with grammar decoding), but collapses catastrophically when a modality is missing at test time ($25.18\,\%$ without video on clean speech; $75.31\,\%$ without audio).
  \item \textbf{Proposed Model~P (P4, initialised from B1 + B2 v2)} achieves our best overall greedy test WER (\textbf{1.02\,\%} clean, \textbf{2.51\,\%} mean across SNRs) and best clean grammar WER (\textbf{0.71\,\%}, $0.34\,\%$ CER). Crucially, Model~P resolves B3's missing-modality fragility via $\mathbf{e}_{\text{miss}}$ and dynamic gating: dropping video yields \textbf{1.88\,\%} clean WER (matching standalone B1's $1.96\,\%$), dropping audio yields \textbf{23.78\,\%--31.57\,\%} WER (matching standalone B2), and the mean fusion gate $\bar{g}$ automatically shifts from $0.804$ (clean) down to $0.691$ ($-5$\,dB) to rely more heavily on lip movements as acoustic noise intensifies.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Why v2 Used ResNet-18, Modality Ablations, and Grammar Decoding}
\label{sec:v2}

\subsection{Why ResNet-18 (\code{v2}) Outperforms 3D-CNN (\code{v1}) in Multimodal Fusion}
Although standalone \textbf{B2 v2} (\code{resnet18\_bilstm}, capped at a 50-minute / 13-epoch budget with aggressive spatial cutout and time masking) recorded a higher standalone WER ($32.20\,\%$) than 28-epoch \textbf{B2 v1} ($23.04\,\%$), our controlled 30-minute full-corpus comparison between \textbf{Model P3} (\code{B1 + B2 v1 Conv3D}) and \textbf{Model P4} (\code{B1 + B2 v2 ResNet-18}) proves that the \code{ResNet-18} visual encoder provides substantially superior representations for multimodal fusion:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{52.1\,\% lower clean test WER and 38.0\,\% lower validation WER:} Holding the audio checkpoint (\code{B1}), fusion module, learning rate, and 30-minute training budget identical, swapping \code{B2 v1} (\code{Conv3D}) for \code{B2 v2} (\code{ResNet-18}) cuts validation WER from $14.17\,\%$ to \textbf{8.82\,\%} and greedy test WER from $2.13\,\%$ to \textbf{1.02\,\%} (clean) and from $7.30\,\%$ to \textbf{5.34\,\%} ($-5$\,dB).
  \item \textbf{Architectural reason:} \code{v1} flattens a $96{\times}6{\times}6 = 3{,}456$ feature map after only 3 plain convolutions, making it sensitive to speaker lighting and crop shifts. \code{v2} combines a 5-frame 3D spatiotemporal stem (\code{Conv3d} $5{\times}7{\times}7$) with per-clip zero-mean/unit-variance normalisation (\code{input\_norm="clip"}), a 16-layer 2D residual trunk with skip connections, and global \code{AdaptiveAvgPool2d(1)}, producing speaker-invariant viseme features (detailed in our companion note \code{resnet\_vs\_conv3d\_justification.pdf}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Missing-Modality Robustness and Noise-Adaptive Gating (Model P vs.\ B3 v2)}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} compares \textbf{B3 v2} and \textbf{Model P} when an entire modality is dropped at test time (\code{--drop-modality visual} or \code{--drop-modality audio}) alongside Model P4's mean fusion gate $\bar{g}_t = \sigma(\mathbf{w}^\top [\tilde{\mathbf{a}}_t; \tilde{\mathbf{v}}_t] + b)$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Graceful unimodal fallback:} When video is dropped, \textbf{B3 v2} degrades from $1.16\,\%$ to $25.18\,\%$ WER (clean), whereas \textbf{Model P4} substitutes $\mathbf{e}_{\text{miss}}$ and achieves \textbf{1.88\,\%} clean WER---matching standalone B1 ($1.96\,\%$). When audio is dropped, B3 v2 collapses to $75.31\,\%$, whereas Model~P maintains $23.78\,\%$ (P2), $25.65\,\%$ (P3), and $31.57\,\%$ (P4, beating standalone B2 v2's $32.20\,\%$).
  \item \textbf{Verified noise-adaptive gate shift:} As SNR drops from $\text{clean}$ to $-5$\,dB, Model P4's mean gate $\bar{g}$ decreases monotonically from \textbf{0.804} to \textbf{0.691} ($\Delta\bar{g} = -0.113$, more than $2.2{\times}$ the shift of P3's \code{Conv3D} gate from $0.884 \to 0.834$), shifting up to $30.9\,\%$ fusion weight onto the visual stream under heavy noise.
\end{itemize}
