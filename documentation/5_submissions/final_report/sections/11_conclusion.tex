\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On GRID, lip-assisted fusion reduced errors at every noise level. P4 lowered the WER of the
audio-only baseline on four unseen speakers from 1.96~\% to 1.02~\% on clean audio and from
9.45~\% to 5.34~\% at $-5$~dB, and its gate shifted towards the lips as the SNR fell. Modality
dropout and the 3-D lip front end contributed most; attention with gating added a smaller but
consistent gain, and a concatenation model built from the same pretrained encoders came within a few
tenths of a point.

On real speech, the gain carried over in noise but not on clean audio. Against an audio-only twin,
ws\_v5 reduced WER at $-10$~dB from 42.3~\% to 28.5~\% on TED and from 64.5~\% to 49.2~\% on films,
and cost 1.3 points on clean TED speech. It failed when the audio did not belong to the visible
speaker, and retraining on such audio cost more on clean speech than it recovered. Future work
should give the fusion gate an explicit audio-visual synchrony signal.
