\section{Data and Noise}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{GRID corpus}
LipScribe is trained and tested on the GRID audio-visual corpus~\cite{cooke2006}. Every GRID
sentence follows a six-slot command grammar (command, colour, preposition, letter, digit,
adverb) drawn from 51 words, and lasts 3~s: 75 video frames at 25~frames/s with 16~kHz audio.
The team verified every utterance before use (\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak{}verify\_\allowbreak{}dataset.py}): video,
audio and alignment files present; $75 \pm 2$ decodable frames; audio duration within 40~ms of
the video; a six-word transcript from the GRID word list; audio neither silent nor clipped
throughout; and a Face Mesh success rate of at least 90~\% of frames.

The split is speaker-independent (\texttt{data/\allowbreak{}splits.csv}), so no test speaker is ever seen in
training: 26 speakers for training (25,891 usable utterances), 3 for validation (3,000) and 4
for testing (3,999), 32,890 in total. Speaker s21 is excluded because its video is missing.
The split was fixed in Week~1 and never regenerated.

\subsection{Noise}
Background noise comes from the MUSAN corpus~\cite{snyder2015}. Its 930 noise files are split
by file into separate pools for training (651), validation (139) and testing (140), so no noise
recording appears in more than one pool. During training each utterance is mixed with a random
training-pool segment at a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) drawn uniformly from clean, 10, 5, 0 and
$-5$~dB. Validation and test use fixed noisy copies of every utterance at the same five levels,
made once with deterministic seeds (\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak{}make\_\allowbreak{}noisy\_\allowbreak{}test.py}), so every model is
scored on identical audio. After mixing, each waveform is scaled to $-25$~dBFS RMS with the
gain capped at 20~dB, so near-silent clips are not amplified into pure noise.

\subsection{Real-world data}
% Drafted by Charis Opol on 23 Nov for Faith to check. Sources: Week 4 report (mining pipeline),
% reports/flix_v4/decision_log.md (relabelling) and reports/flix_v5/decision_log.md (manifests).
The real-world clips are mined from films, series and TED talks by
\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak{}mine\_\allowbreak{}flix.py}, which writes them in the same format as GRID: 25~frames/s mouth
crops, 16~kHz audio and a transcript. A candidate line is kept only if the video shows exactly one
large, frontal face, the lips move and there is no camera cut, so only about 7 to 8~\% of
candidate lines survive. The rest show slides, the audience, two people, a turned head or a cut.
Subtitled films are labelled by their subtitles; films without subtitles and all TED talks are
labelled with Whisper large-v3-turbo word timestamps~\cite{radford2023whisper}. TED subtitle times
did not match the cut clips, so on 13~Nov every TED clip was relabelled from its own audio. TED
talks are harvested by downloading, mining and deleting each talk in turn, so only the mouth crops
and the clip audio are kept.

The test titles (three films) and 67 test talks are never used for training, validation or the
language models. After the v5 re-cut (Section~\ref{sec:pipelines-real}) and the removal of clips
with no speech, unspellable text, Whisper hallucinations or labels too long for CTC, the sets hold
37,634 training clips from films and TED, 805 TED and 67 film validation clips, and 1,094 TED and
41 film test clips (\texttt{reports/\allowbreak{}flix\_\allowbreak{}v5/\allowbreak{}decision\_\allowbreak{}log.md}). MUSAN noise is again added at test
time, at clean, 0, $-5$ and $-10$~dB.
