\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textit{GRID is a narrow benchmark.} Its sentences follow one six-slot grammar over 51 words, its
speakers face the camera in a studio, and our test set has only four speakers. The GRID results
show that fusion helps under controlled conditions, not that it helps in a lecture hall. Our
confidence intervals resample sentences, so they do not capture how much the gain varies from one
speaker to another; with four speakers, that cannot be estimated.

\textit{The noise is synthetic.} All noise is MUSAN audio added at a fixed SNR. Real rooms add
reverberation, distance from the microphone and people talking at the same time. Babble and
competing speech are the hardest cases for audio-only recognition~\cite{shi2022robust}, and we test
them only in the wrong-audio conditions of Section~\ref{sec:realworld}.

\textit{The real-world references come from Whisper.} TED and most film labels are Whisper
transcripts of the clip's own audio, so real-world WER measures agreement with Whisper rather than
with a human transcript. This is also why no Whisper-based encoder was used. The film test set has
only 41 clips from three films, and its intervals are wide.

\textit{Single runs.} Each GRID model and ablation was trained once, within a fixed budget.
Differences of a few tenths of a point, such as P4 against B3~v2, may change with another seed.

\textit{Edge words.} ws\_v5 still gets a clip's first or last word wrong about three times as
often as an inner word (30.6~\% against 10.8~\%), even after the clips were re-cut with 0.32~s of
padding. Live captioning cuts speech into windows, so the same problem will appear at every window
boundary unless the windows overlap.

\textit{Voices that do not belong to the face.} ws\_v5 trusts any audio it hears, so it transcribes
a second speaker or a dubbed voice as if the person on screen said it. Training on mismatched audio
(ws\_v6) fixed this only by trusting the audio less everywhere. A more promising route is an explicit
audio-visual synchrony signal, such as a SyncNet-style score~\cite{chung2017}, given to the gate as
one more reliability input, in the way AV-RelScore scores each stream~\cite{hong2023}.

\textit{Frontal faces and visual corruption.} Every training clip shows one large, frontal face.
Turned heads, hands over the mouth, masks and poor light are not tested, and the lips can be
corrupted as well as the audio~\cite{hong2023}. P4 falls back to $e_{\text{miss}}$ only when the face
is lost in more than half of a clip's frames.

\textit{Latency.} The browser demo returns a caption in 2 to 3~s on our GPU workstation. Latency and
real-time factor on a CPU or a phone have not yet been measured (F-08) \textbf{[AQ3]}.
